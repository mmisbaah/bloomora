# Child playtesting

Automated checks cover correctness and failure paths. They cannot establish whether children understand the instructions or enjoy the game.

With a parent or teacher supervising, observe at least one child in each age band using a fresh local profile. Do not coach the first attempt or record identifying information.

1. Ask them to start a level and explain what they think they should do.
2. Observe a mistake: can they understand the picture and correct their answer?
3. Ask them to find help. Check whether clue, example and guidance make sense.
4. Let them try relaxed mode. Ask which mode they prefer and why.
5. After a level, ask what changed in the lagoon and what they want to do next.
6. For older children, ask them to explain a rule comparison and create a related pattern.
7. On a later day, try a three-puzzle Practice Pool session.

Record age band, device size, where assistance was needed, misleading wording, mistaken taps and whether they chose to continue. Prioritize repeated confusion over requests for more rewards. Keep these notes separate from children's progress codes.
